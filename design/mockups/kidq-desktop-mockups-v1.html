KidQ — Desktop-First Responsive Pivot

Rounds 1–2: the watching screen, its cast-sender pairing, and the login set (who's-watching, no-session with yesterday-replay) plus the after-break choice screen from player-flow feedback, built as one responsive system (CSS container queries, not fixed frames) and checked at three widths. The sky-as-timer stays a true arc at every width — the earlier plan to flatten it into the day-progress-bar was reconsidered after review; the arc now just widens and flattens its curvature gradually, the way a real horizon does, while the day-bar stays underneath exactly as it does on phone.

Same markup, same CSS classes, no per-device duplication — resizing a browser window would move a real page between these three states. Content is illustrative (gradient placeholders stand in for the real BBB clip stills used elsewhere in the mock set).

Same screen, three widths

The phone frame is pixel-identical to the locked mock in kidq-mockups-v1.html — the parity check. Tablet and wide use the same element set, just rearranged and rescaled.

Same width, full session range

Pressure-testing the arc itself: does the sun's position still read as "where we are in the day" once the arc is this wide? Three moments from the same session, laptop width.

Reading the range: the sun's height above the horizon line still tells the story at a glance — low-and-rising at the start, cresting mid-session, dipping back toward the horizon under a dusk wash in the last minute — the same shape as the phone version, just wider. The day-progress-bar underneath tracks the same three moments in parallel, the way it already does on phone.

Cast-sender pairing

Whichever device is casting — phone or laptop — becomes this: sky-as-clock and remote, no player card, one pause button. Same arc and sun components as the watching screen above, same phone/laptop parity.

Who's watching — the child's front door

New in round 2: the login pairing. Parent setup happened in the parent app; the child's only "login" is touching their own face. Pre-dawn sky — story-wise this sits before the sunrise screen: pick who you are, then your sun rises. Shown on every open (shared devices are the norm). No parent-facing controls anywhere in child mode — picking and settings live in the parent app.

No session yet — now with yesterday

The phone set's sleeping-sun screen, joining the desktop system — with one addition from group feedback: a replay of yesterday's parent-picked session when one exists. A memory, not a library: one touch, one normal finite session, no browsing.

After the break — choose from Mumma-Papa's picks

From group feedback on the player flow, adopted as choice within the parent's basket: when the break ends and the sun is back in its seat, the child can touch the sun for the next video in order — or touch any of the remaining parent-picked cards to watch that one instead. No recommendations, no algorithm, nothing new enters the queue; "The End 🌙" stays where it is. (This deliberately reopens the earlier "child has no queue control" decision — approved for reaction.) Session shown: 4 videos, 2 remaining.

Open items not addressed in this round (logged in the spec, deferred on purpose): the Cast receiver's ambient-backdrop gap, cast-connect entry point, sender sleep/resync, browser-tab containment on desktop, and the pause button overlapping the player. Attribution is now dynamic across the set — Mumma, Papa, or both, per video — replacing the old Mumma-only copy. Next: your reaction to rounds 1–2 together; further screens follow the same system.
